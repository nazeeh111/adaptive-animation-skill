# YouTube: promise, production and release

Use only the parts relevant to the request. Editing a supplied clip does not authorize a channel strategy, upload or analytics collection. A new video, release package, Shorts adaptation and performance review are different deliverables. Preserve existing channel identity and native work when supplied; do not invent access, audience data or growth guarantees.

## Idea and execution

Establish who would choose this video and what they should gain or feel. Connect the premise, working title/thumbnail, opening and actual payoff. Package the content honestly; a question earns attention when the video answers it. Choose the structure from the material: a demonstration, argument, investigation, tutorial, essay, conversation, performance or another appropriate form. No mandatory hook length, cut frequency, voice, visual style or template.

For research-led work, distinguish evidence, inference and opinion; retain sources for material claims. Outline the causal/story progression, needed footage or drawings, spoken text and sound only as far as useful before production. A transcript helps select moments but does not establish factual accuracy, performance or context. Preserve qualifications and speaker intent when condensing. Plan the strongest feasible treatment with available inputs rather than padding a weak premise with effects or narration.

For a Shorts/vertical adaptation, choose a self-contained idea and readable composition at actual phone size. Recompose important subjects and captions where needed; avoid automatic center crops that remove the action. Check current classification, duration, thumbnail and feature rules against official sources when they affect delivery. Do not silently speed, loop or pad material to fit remembered limits.

## Edit and package

Use [editing guidance](editorial.md) for the actual cut. Treat title/thumbnail as a coherent invitation that the video fulfills. When alternatives are useful, vary the proposition or focus, not merely colors. Judge thumbnail subject recognition, hierarchy and text at small display size; retain native design source and necessary assets. An export-size check does not establish visual quality or audience response.

Deliver what was requested: selected video, editable project/cut instructions, thumbnail, chosen title and useful alternatives, description/credits/sources and captions/chapters when relevant. Keep local release material plainly marked as a draft. Caption speech and meaningful sound accurately; never invent dialogue for a silent film. Chapters must refer to actual final timings and suit the video rather than satisfy a template. Keep licenses/provenance and disclosure decisions tied to actual inputs, not a blanket assumption about all AI-assisted work. Resolve required channel/audience/account settings from supplied facts or the authorized live interface before publication.

Verify the selected media's actual format, duration, frame rate, full decode, audio, crop/text and caption timing. Check graphics over the intended background and target size. Do not re-encode a healthy master solely to hit a remembered recommended bitrate; confirm the current delivery contract, source quality and any real incompatibility first. Preserve the master and identify derivatives. A local-ready package is not proof that YouTube accepted, processed or published it.

## Feedback and changes

Use authorized analytics or supplied exports only when performance analysis is in scope. Interpret retention, impressions/click-through, watch time, traffic source and audience segments together. A rewatch spike can signal interest or confusion; a dip can reflect skipping or abandonment. Compare like periods and relevant audiences; avoid causal conclusions from a small uncontrolled before/after change. Native title/thumbnail experiments may be useful when currently eligible, but neither a clear winner nor viral growth is guaranteed. Choose a testable edit or packaging hypothesis instead of changing everything from one metric.
