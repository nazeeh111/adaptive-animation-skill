# Choose the animation language, not a house style

Start from what the audience should see happen and what must remain editable. Select the look, motion logic and medium separately. A paper world can have smooth motion or deliberately stepped poses; a scientific diagram can be restrained or expressive. The bundled title study and fonts are optional materials. Do not carry its colors, split layout, typography or reveal into unrelated requests.

Record only useful direction: subject-specific visual idea; references and what to learn from them; shape/material/color/type language; camera/pose rhythm; key action; delivery and editable source. Choose unspecified details yourself. A reference is evidence of a desired quality, not permission to copy its assets, artist identity or entire composition.

## Develop the idea before extending the pipeline

Translate a reference into observable choices: what changes, what holds, where attention goes, which materials/shapes carry identity, how camera and sound change the reading. Avoid mood adjectives without an executable visual consequence. If a route is familiar but the output feels generic, change the organizing idea or staging before adding a new tool.

When exploration will change the result, compare a small number of meaningfully different directions. For example, a product's operation could be staged through a continuous transformation, a cause-and-effect demonstration or a character's consequence. These are possibilities, not required genres or a fixed three-option ritual. Use the actual subject and inputs; do not invent product behavior to justify a concept. Resolve uncertain movement in a tiny animatic or pose sequence, then choose a direction and finish it. Discarded studies need no production pipeline.

For improving existing work, compare the affected before/after at the same output size and timing context. Ask whether the important action is clearer or more compelling, whether the subject retains identity and whether the revision introduces distracting motion or polish that hides the idea. Preserve the prior result. Technical validity and a richer control rig do not substitute for a stronger performance or image.

## Preserve visual continuity

For recurring subjects, keep the few fragile visual constants in the existing
project note or board: distinctive features/proportions, relative scale, material
and palette, geography/eye lines, light and current prop/action state as relevant.
Reuse the authoritative assets or reference images across shots and generated
takes. Record motivated changes such as damage, wardrobe, time or deliberate
style shifts; consistency does not mean identical framing or frozen appearance.

Compare actual outgoing/incoming frames and a representative moving interval
against those references, including after a revision or color/crop change. Look
for feature drift, unexplained scale/light changes, reversed spatial relationships
and state resets. The same asset, prompt or seed alone does not clear the result.
Fix the cause in the affected source, then check connected shots. A small
side-by-side comparison is enough; no new scene schema or continuity service.

## Technique selection

These are approaches to choose and verify for a brief, not a claim that every technique has been locally proven.

Do not confuse a style label with its production method. Cel-shaded3D, individually exposed drawings, a cutout rig and a stepped clay render have different editable sources and motion behavior. Prove the method the brief actually needs. Retained examples separately exercise native rig/contact, continuous organic skinning and code-authored vector cels. None establishes human drawing, motion capture or all-style mastery. Use [character guidance](character.md) for contact, binding, deformation and exposure lessons.

| Requested language or behavior | Practical authoring route | What determines success |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drawn, cut-paper, collage, ink, painterly or textured2D | Native drawing/paint/vector tools, SVG/Canvas/HTML layers or supplied/generated raster assets as appropriate; Grease Pencil when drawing and3D staging help | A coherent drawing/material language, readable silhouettes, layer depth and intentional poses; raster assets need provenance |
| Frame-by-frame, cel animation or anime-inspired timing | Native drawings/sprites/Grease Pencil or code-drawn poses; explicit exposure sheet for which drawings appear on which frames | Key poses, breakdowns, contact and readable held drawings; avoid substituting smooth object tweens for the requested drawing behavior |
| Pixel animation, sprite sheets, low-resolution game motion | Small integer-grid Canvas/sprites, palette rules, nearest-neighbor scaling, timed native frame sequence | Crisp output pixels without unintended interpolation; actual requested sprite states/layout/loop contract |
| Stop motion, clay, miniature or paper craft | Blender materials/lights/camera or real image sequences, deliberate stepped animation and measured physical imperfections | Consistent scale/contact/lighting; stepping cadence chosen by action, with source frames/rig preserved |
| Character acting, creatures, walk cycles, lip sync | Blender native rig/shape keys/actions, Grease Pencil or a small2D rig; measured voice/phoneme cues as needed | Poses and emotion, planted contacts, weight, facial timing, anatomy/style consistency; character craft needs its own short test |
| Cinematic3D, stylized, architectural, product or photoreal | Native Blender geometry/materials/lighting/cameras; use existing renderer or tested alternate when required | Visual reference, materials, exposure/color, camera intent, samples/noise, usable render cost; photoreal quality is judged from the image |
| Procedural, generative, particles, fluid, cloth or destruction | Native Blender simulation/geometry nodes or deterministic browser simulation/shaders for appropriate scale | Stable seeds, actual dynamics/contact, bounded memory, baked caches and restart; a label is not a physical simulation |
| Kinetic type, logos, diagrams, product/UI or abstract motion | Browser GSAP/SVG/Canvas; ManimGX for precise geometry/math; native3D if depth/materials matters | Type/shape integrity, meaningful timing, identity through transitions, final format safe areas |
| Scientific/data/engineering | ManimGX and numerical source; browser when UI/interactive structure is central | Correct units, equations, geometry and causal progression, with assumptions made explicit |
| Footage/VFX/compositing, tracking, rotoscope or grade | Resolve/Fusion or Blender compositor/trackers, suitable installed specialized tools if needed | Actual source footage, mask/track stability, edges/grain/light/color and receiving-side native project checks |
| Interactive, web, looping vector or game-ready animation | Browser native source; SVG sequences or native rig/action export for the requested runtime | State/event behavior, replay/seek, performance/accessibility and actual receiving-runtime import; MP4 alone is not an interactive asset |

Several rows can describe one shot. Combine only where useful. Do not install a separate generator per style or flatten all output into a universal scene graph. Prefer the tools already present and preserve their native files. If a key capability is missing, inspect installed capabilities, choose a feasible free route and run a small relevant proof before committing the whole production. Do not describe a tool listing, synthetic demo or untested exporter as production coverage.

## Make movement legible

A time-driven animation can include anticipation, weight shifts, squash/stretch, follow-through, arcs, overshoot, contact, secondary action, camera motion and holds. Select these for the subject; none is mandatory in every scene. Prioritize the action silhouette and trajectory before particles, blur, grading or transitions. A simulation, pose animation and illustration have different standards of correctness.

For offline code-drawn capture that requires backward seeking or independent frames, derive drawing from scene time and seeded inputs, or replay a fixed event log/baked state. Live input, interactive state and physical simulation may legitimately evolve; define their input/state/time contract rather than banning stateful animation. Frame-by-frame work should retain an exposure sheet or sequence index, not rely on real-time timers. For authored rigs, preserve constraints/actions and bake only for a specific exporter when required. Keep handmade variation intentional and repeatable rather than randomizing every capture.

Prove the risky part in motion: a walk that plants its feet, a cloth contact, a seamless loop, the desired line quality, a shader, a transparent edge or an export into the target app. Then finish the actual brief. Do not replace production with a catalog of demonstrations, impose one aesthetic, or promise universal perfection from a small proof.
